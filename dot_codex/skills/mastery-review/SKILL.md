# Mastery Review

Measure what the learner can retrieve and use without support, then target review to observed gaps. Do not confuse familiarity, confidence, or rereading with mastery.

## Choose evidence of mastery

Match the assessment to the goal. Sample several kinds of evidence when appropriate:

- Recall: state definitions, invariants, mechanisms, or procedures from memory.
- Explanation: justify why something works and distinguish nearby concepts.
- Prediction: trace code, systems behavior, equations, or experiments before seeing results.
- Application: solve a representative problem with minimal cues.
- Transfer: use the idea in a novel context or compare alternative designs.
- Debugging: locate and explain a plausible misconception or failure.

Begin with a small diagnostic rather than a long generic quiz. Ask one question at a time in interactive sessions, unless the user requests a worksheet or complete practice set. Avoid trivia unless exact recall is genuinely required.

## Evaluate and remediate

After each response, record the demonstrated strength and the precise gap. Calibrate feedback as correct, partially correct, incorrect, or unsupported by enough evidence, and explain why. Confidence should influence what to probe, not whether an answer counts as correct.

For a gap, choose the smallest effective intervention: a cue, contrast, counterexample, prerequisite repair, brief reteaching, or targeted practice. Then reassess with a different question; do not count immediate repetition of the same answer as durable learning.

## Review plan

When a plan is requested, prioritize observed weaknesses and high-value prerequisites. Include:

- concrete learning outcomes stated as observable performances;
- short retrieval sessions distributed over time rather than one long rereading block;
- mixed practice once individual skills are stable;
- increasing delay and difficulty as recall improves;
- a criterion for advancing, revisiting, or retiring each topic.

Use calendar dates only when the user provides a deadline or schedule. Otherwise describe intervals flexibly. Finish with a concise mastery map: secure areas, developing areas, gaps, and the next best practice task. Do not claim durable mastery from a single successful response.
