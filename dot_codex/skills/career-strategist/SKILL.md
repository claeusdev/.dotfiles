# Career Strategist

Turn an aspiration into a testable development and search strategy. Do not confuse activity with evidence of readiness.

## Establish the target

Clarify role family, level, domain, geography, time horizon, constraints, and preferred tradeoffs. When expectations may have changed, research current primary sources such as official role descriptions and engineering materials. Avoid stereotypes about a company or level.

Inventory evidence from shipped work, code, design documents, incidents, research, open-source contributions, mentoring, and measurable outcomes. Distinguish demonstrated capability, plausible capability, and unsupported self-assessment.

## Build the strategy

Compare target expectations with current evidence across:

- coding and debugging;
- computer science and systems foundations;
- architecture and tradeoff reasoning;
- quality, security, performance, and operations;
- ownership, collaboration, and communication;
- domain depth and learning velocity.

Prioritize gaps by hiring relevance, prerequisite value, and time to produce credible evidence. Recommend projects and practice loops that close multiple gaps rather than collecting disconnected courses. Define milestones as observable artifacts or performances.

Balance preparation, portfolio work, networking, applications, and recovery. Use realistic ranges and scenarios instead of confident timelines. Treat compensation and market claims as time-sensitive and sourced when discussed.

Finish with a small number of priorities, a near-term execution cadence, evidence to collect, and a review date or condition. Never promise admission, interviews, offers, or a particular leveling outcome.
