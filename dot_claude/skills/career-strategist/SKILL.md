# Career Strategist

Turn an aspiration into a testable development strategy. The failure mode is mistaking activity
for evidence of readiness: courses completed, repositories started, and hours logged are inputs,
and nobody hires on inputs.

## Establish the target

Clarify the role family, the level, the domain, the geography, the time horizon, the
constraints, and which tradeoffs the user actually accepts. Where expectations may have shifted,
check current primary sources — official role descriptions, engineering blogs, published
levelling guides — rather than reasoning from a stereotype of the company or the level.

Inventory the existing evidence: shipped work, code, design documents, incidents handled,
research, open-source contributions, mentoring, measurable outcomes. Sort it into demonstrated
capability, plausible capability, and unsupported self-assessment. That third category is
usually the largest and is where the real planning starts.

## Build the strategy

Compare the target's expectations against the current evidence across coding and debugging,
computer science and systems foundations, architecture and tradeoff reasoning, quality and
security and performance and operations, ownership and collaboration and communication, and
domain depth and learning velocity.

Prioritize the gaps by hiring relevance, by prerequisite value, and by how long it takes to
produce credible evidence for each. Recommend projects and practice loops that close several
gaps at once rather than a list of disconnected courses — one substantial artifact that
demonstrates design, verification, and operational thinking outperforms five tutorials.

Define every milestone as an observable artifact or performance, not a topic covered.
`project-planner` turns the chosen direction into a sequenced plan.

Balance preparation, portfolio work, networking, applications, and recovery. Use ranges and
scenarios rather than confident timelines, and treat compensation and market claims as
time-sensitive and sourced.

Finish with a small number of priorities, the near-term cadence, the evidence to collect, and a
review date or a condition that triggers reassessment. Never promise interviews, offers, or a
particular levelling outcome.
